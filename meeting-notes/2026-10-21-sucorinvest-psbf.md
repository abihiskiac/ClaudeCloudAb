# Meeting with Sucorinvest – Sucor Premium Sovereign Balanced Fund (PSBF)

21 October 2026

## Market backdrop

- At the end of 2025 the bond market was rich, with demand running ahead of supply. It was a seller's market, and buyers were accepting lower yields on investment-grade bonds, especially those rated A to AAA.

## Positioning and recent drawdown

- PSBF has been positioned defensively. Government bond duration has been kept short, and the equity book sits in defensive names, mainly financials, where downside is seen as limited.
- PSBF was still hit by the recent correction. The fund did not hold an ample reserve, and the shock across both the bond and equity markets was too large to absorb. The fund was not positioned for a move of that size.
- To keep duration short and build liquidity, Sucor has not been rolling over maturing bonds in full. Only part of the principal is reinvested, for example around 25% of the maturing amount.

## AUM and fund size

- AUM grew significantly between March and September 2026. The inflows were used to buy bonds, both PBS and corporate bonds.
- The fund is still a manageable size at under IDR 1 tn. Government bonds are around IDR 300 bn and equity is of a similar size, so positions are easy to trade.
- Sucor trades actively and is willing to take contrarian positions. The small AUM makes this easier.

## Duration strategy

- PBS make up around 40% of the fund. As they mature, the proceeds will go back into FR/PBS with slightly longer duration.
- Duration is currently around 1.90 years. If the market recovers it could move to around 2 years.
- Sucor will manage duration more actively. Each maturing position will be reviewed ahead of its maturity date to decide what market conditions justify re-entering and into which bonds. If conditions look like they do today, the fund will stay defensive.

## Corporate bonds

- Corporate bonds used to be very illiquid, with thin bids and offers. Liquidity has improved and pricing is more efficient, but price volatility is now higher.
- PHEI plans to change its pricing range. Funds that are heavy in corporate bonds are expected to become more volatile as a result.
- Sector selection in corporate bonds is active. Most AAA and AA supply comes from multifinance companies, which need to refinance every year. Conventional corporates mainly issue to fund capex such as new plants or expansion, and they are currently holding back because rates are high and spreads are unattractive. The fund's multifinance weighting therefore reflects what paper is available rather than a sector preference.
