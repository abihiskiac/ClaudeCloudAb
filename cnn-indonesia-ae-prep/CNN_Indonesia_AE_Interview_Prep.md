## 2. The job: what a CNN Indonesia Account Executive actually does

### The sales cycle, end to end
1. **Prospecting and account planning.** You cover two kinds of buyer:
   - **Media agencies**, which plan and buy media for big brands. The agency planner shortlists channels; the buyer negotiates price.
   - **Direct clients**: brands, state-owned enterprises (BUMN), ministries and institutions, regional governments and corporates.
2. **Brief to proposal.** Read the client's brief (objective, target audience, period, budget, KPIs). Build a proposal with the programming, digital, creative and events teams.
3. **Negotiation.** Rate card vs. discount, bonus spots, added value (such as digital or social amplification), package pricing.
4. **Booking and execution.** Purchase order or insertion order, ad material (*materi iklan*), schedule, coordination with traffic and production, script approval for built-ins and advertorials.
5. **Proof and reporting.** Proof of airing (*bukti tayang* or broadcast log), digital reports (impressions, views, CTR), post-campaign report.
6. **Billing and collection.** Invoice, tax invoice (*faktur pajak*), follow-up on the payment term (TOP). Many media companies judge AEs on **collected** revenue, not just booked revenue. That's a natural strength for a banker.
7. **Retention and upsell.** Plan next quarter, invite clients to events, renew annual deals.

## 4. Industry context for 2025–2026

### Market size and mix (different sources measure differently)
- **WPP Media (GroupM):** Indonesian ad market expected to grow **9.5% to US$6.445 billion in 2025**, with **digital about 75%** and **TV about 16%**.
- **dentsu:** expected growth of **5.1% in 2025**. Digital up 10–12%, TV holding steady at +3–5%, outdoor and digital outdoor up about 7%.
- **PwC Outlook 2026–2030:** Indonesian advertising grows from **US$12.8 billion (2025) to US$18.9 billion (2030)**. This uses a broader definition.
- **Telkom:** expects the digital ad market to reach **Rp140 trillion by 2028**.

### Nielsen measures gross rate card
**Nielsen measures ad spend at gross rate-card value**, before discounts, packages and bonuses. That's why Nielsen reports show TV dominating (79.7% of spend in H1 2022), while agency net-spend estimates show digital dominating. If an interviewer asks, *"Is TV dying?"*, this lets you give a sharp answer: *"Measured at gross rate card, TV still looks dominant; in real money, digital has overtaken it. The winning offer combines TV's mass reach and credibility with digital's targeting and measurement, and CNN Indonesia has both."*

### Who's spending (Nielsen, H1 2025)
- **Top categories:** beverages (#1), laptops & smartphones, food. These categories dominate TV, digital and outdoor.
- **Big advertisers:** Nestlé, Indofood, Ajinomoto Indonesia, Aqua, Tempo Scan Pacific. Tech players **GoTo** and **Garena** are in the top 10.

### Government spending fell
- **Inpres 1/2025** cut government spending. For example, West Java's provincial media ad budget reportedly fell from **Rp50 billion to Rp3.5 billion**.
- **RRI and TVRI** laid off more than 1,000 contributors.
- TV stations cut programmes and aired more reruns. The broadcasters' association (ATVSI) noted that advertising is TV's main revenue source while costs stay fixed.
- Dewan Pers observed that government ad money is moving to **social media and creators**.
- **The takeaway for an AE:** government and institutional money still exists but has to be earned. Diversify into private-sector categories.

### The economy in 2026, through a banker's eyes (your edge)
- **GDP:** +5.29% year on year in Q2 2026, down from 5.61% in Q1. Mining was the only sector that contracted.
- **BI rate:** 5.75% after **100 bps of hikes in 2026**. Some economists see 6.25% by year-end.
- **Rupiah:** down more than **7.4% year to date** (as of Aug 2026).
- **What this means for advertisers:**
  - Importers and FMCG companies face higher costs, so marketing budgets get tighter and more accountable.
  - Banks and multifinance companies keep advertising, but with ROI pressure.
  - Commodity-linked regions such as Kalimantan feel mining weakness.
- **What it means for selling:** focus on measurable packages, flexible payment terms for strong clients, and **checking direct clients' ability to pay**. You already do credit analysis, so say so.

### Agency landscape (global moves that shape the local market)
- **Omnicom completed its acquisition of IPG on 26 Nov 2025.** Omnicom Media is now the biggest media agency group globally.
- **WPP Media** (formerly GroupM) led new-business wins in Q1 2026.
- **dentsu** is running a strategic review of its international operations.
- **Locally, this means** agency consolidation, centralised buying and pressure on price. AEs win by offering **ideas and integrated packages**, not just discounts.

### So what? Five talking points for the interview
1. **Credible news is a brand-safe environment.** Advertisers worry about appearing next to hoaxes or controversy, and CNN's brand standards reduce that risk.
2. **Sell integrated, not spots.** Combine TV credibility, digital targeting, YouTube reach (about 12M subscribers) and events.
3. **Events and IP are a revenue engine**, but diversify beyond government budgets.
4. **Bring data.** Pair TV reach (TVR, GRP) with digital KPIs (views, clicks, leads) in every proposal.
5. **Category ideas where you have an edge:**
   - Banking and fintech: your industry. Note the possible conflict of interest with BCA and handle it professionally.
   - Automotive: your Auto Show experience.
   - FMCG beverages and food: the biggest spenders.
   - Telco, devices and e-commerce.
   - BUMN.
   - Kalimantan regional businesses and governments.

## 5. The hiring process: what to expect

No CNN Indonesia AE interview reports are public. This is the **typical Trans group process** from applicant accounts, which are anecdotal and some are years old. Your company's steps may differ.

1. **Online application and CV screening.**
2. **Psychological test (*psikotes*).**
   - A Trans7 applicant reported the **Wartegg** test (completing drawings from small marks), a **tree drawing** and a **person drawing**.
   - A Trans TV programme test used **standard psychometric tests plus general-knowledge and English tests**, without the Pauli/Kraepelin arithmetic test.
3. **HR interview:** motivation, attitude, culture fit, availability, salary expectations.
4. **User interview** with the sales manager or head: your sales track record, how you'd sell, and possibly a **role-play or pitch**.
5. **Final interview** (sometimes, with a director), then **offer and medical check-up**.
6. **Timeline:** one Trans7 applicant said the whole process took **close to two months**. Glassdoor's average for CNN globally is 38 days. Don't panic if things go quiet.

**Language:** expect mostly **Bahasa Indonesia**, but be ready to switch to English. It's a CNN brand, and your CV says IELTS 7.5, so they may test it. Prepare your intro in both languages (Section 6).

**Psychological test tips:** Wartegg, tree and person drawings have no right answers. Draw complete, well-proportioned, well-placed drawings (a whole tree with roots, a whole person with hands and feet) and work steadily. For arithmetic tests (Pauli/Kraepelin), keep a consistent pace and accuracy rather than rushing then fading. Sleep well beforehand.

**If the interview is online:** good light, neutral background, camera at eye level, stable connection, earphones, a printed copy of your CV and notes beside the screen. Join 5 minutes early.

**If in person:** business formal (blazer). Arrive 15–20 minutes early, because Jakarta traffic is unpredictable. Bring printed CVs, your KTP, and copies of your degree certificate (*ijazah*) and transcript. Indonesian companies often ask for them.

6. **What are your strengths?** Pick three with proof: target delivery (IDR figures), analysis of client finances, building events and relationships.
7. **What's your weakness?** Pick something real but manageable. For example: *"I'm new to media terminology and planning tools. I've already started learning TVR, GRP and CPRP and how agencies buy, and I learn fast; I was best of my batch at BCA."* Or: *"I can over-prepare proposals; I've learned to send a strong first version quickly and refine it with the client."*
8. **Are you comfortable with high, monthly sales targets and pressure?** Yes. You already live with them. Give one example.
9. **Can you relocate to Jakarta? When can you start?** Give a clear, honest answer: your notice period and any service bond (check your contract first).
10. **Where do you see yourself in 3–5 years?** For example: *"A top-performing AE handling key accounts or a category, and growing into a senior AE or sales manager role, ideally leading integrated or event deals."*
11. **What are your salary expectations?** See Section 11.
12. **Do you have other offers or processes?** Be honest and brief. It's fine to say you're selectively exploring.
13. **How do you handle working hours outside 9 to 5?** Client meetings and events happen in the evening. You already run events and gatherings.

### B. Sales competency and behavioural
1. **Walk me through your sales process.** Prospect, qualify (budget, decision-maker, timing), diagnose needs, propose, handle objections, close, deliver, collect, upsell. Tie it to BCA examples.
2. **Tell me about the biggest deal you closed.** STAR story #2.
3. **Tell me about a time you missed a target.** STAR story #7.
4. **How do you find new clients?** Referrals from existing clients, industry events, LinkedIn, tracking who's advertising with competitors, Nielsen data, new product launches, and in this role the **agency planners and buyers**.
5. **How do you prioritise a large portfolio?** Segment by revenue potential and probability; keep weekly pipeline reviews; at BCA you managed 113 clients. Explain how you did it.
6. **How do you handle a client who says your price is too high?** Find the real objection first (budget, value or comparison). Re-anchor on value: audience quality, credibility, integration. Trade rather than give away: *"If we lower price, can we extend the period, or add a commitment?"* Offer options, and know your walk-away point.
7. **How do you handle a client who goes silent after a proposal?** Follow up with something useful (new data, a new idea, an event invitation), ask a direct question about timing, and involve other contacts.
8. **Tell me about a difficult client.** STAR story #8.
9. **How do you work with internal teams** (programming, digital, events, finance)? At BCA, a loan needs credit analysts, approvers and operations. You know how to "sell internally" too.
10. **How do you make sure you get paid?** Your banking edge: check a direct client's ability to pay, agree terms in writing, remind before the due date, escalate politely, keep records, involve the agency's finance team when relevant.
11. **How do you stay motivated after rejection?** Have a short, genuine answer, plus a habit you rely on (for example, reviewing the pipeline and booking the next three meetings).
12. **Give an example of using data to persuade someone.** Credit analysis at BCA, or HubSpot analysis at Glints.

### C. Media sales knowledge (they may test whether you did your homework)
1. **Who are our competitors?** Metro TV, tvOne, Kompas TV, iNews, BTV in news TV; Kompas.com, Tribun, Liputan6, Kumparan, IDN Times online; plus YouTube creators and podcasts competing for the same budgets.
2. **What's the difference between TVR, share, GRP and CPRP?** See the glossary.
   - **TVR:** % of the target audience watching.
   - **Share:** % of people watching TV at that time who are on our channel.
   - **GRP:** sum of the TVRs across a campaign.
   - **CPRP:** spot cost ÷ TVR.
   - **CPM:** cost per 1,000 impressions.
3. **News TV has lower ratings than entertainment. Why should a brand advertise with us?** The audience is more educated, higher-income and includes decision-makers. News carries credibility that rubs off on the brand. Live news creates attention spikes. It's brand-safe compared with user-generated content, it gives access to policymakers through events, and digital and YouTube extend reach.
4. **A client says "TV is dead; we're moving everything to TikTok and creators." What do you say?** Agree that digital matters, and point out that CNN Indonesia *is* digital too (website, about 12M YouTube subscribers, social). Then explain why mixing works: mass reach and credibility plus targeting. Propose a measured test campaign with clear KPIs.
5. **How do agencies decide where to put budget?** The planner builds a media plan against target audience, reach and frequency, and cost efficiency (CPRP, CPM). The buyer negotiates. The client approves. You need relationships with the brand, the planner and the buyer.
6. **What products would you sell to a bank (or car brand, or FMCG brand)?** See Section 8.
7. **What do you know about our events?** CNN Indonesia Awards (national and regional), Leading Women Awards, Agrifood Forum (Section 3).
8. **What advertising trends do you see?** Digital at about 75% of net spend, the push for measurable results, brand safety, agency consolidation, government budget cuts, video and YouTube, creators.
9. **What's an advertorial or branded content, and why does labelling matter?** Paid content in an editorial format. Clear labelling protects reader trust and the brand's credibility; it's an editorial-firewall issue.
10. **Name a recent campaign on our channel you liked.** Do the homework in Section 3 and name one real example.
11. **What do you know about Nielsen ad spend data?** It's measured at gross rate card, so it overstates TV's share compared with net spend (Section 4).
12. **How would you sell during an economic slowdown?** Focus on efficiency and measurable outcomes, offer flexible packages, target sectors that are still growing, and protect cash by checking clients' creditworthiness.

## 8. Worked pitch example (role-play practice)

**Scenario (hypothetical):** A national bank is launching an SME lending product and wants SME owners and decision-makers to know about it within 8 weeks.

**1. Clarify the brief.** Ask: objective (awareness or applications?), target (SME owners, which cities?), period, budget range, KPIs, and any events they already plan.

**2. Insight.** SME owners follow economic news because interest rates and the rupiah affect their costs. In 2026 BI raised rates by 100 bps, so financing is a live topic. A credible news environment makes a bank's message more trustworthy.

**3. Proposed package (illustrative; no real rates):**

| Element | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| TV sponsorship (8 weeks) | Sponsor an economy or business segment: opening and closing billboard, squeeze frame during economic news, short anchor adlib, plus 30s spots in the same programmes | Reach and credibility with business-minded viewers |
| Branded content series | 6-episode "SME success stories" video and article series on cnnindonesia.com and YouTube, clearly labelled as sponsored | Engagement plus measurable views and reads |
| Social amplification | Cut-downs on CNN Indonesia social accounts | Reach younger SME owners |
| On-ground forum | A co-branded SME financing forum (in the style of CNN Indonesia's Agrifood Forum), possibly in Jakarta plus one regional city such as Banjarmasin | Face-to-face leads; PR value |
| Lead capture | QR code or link to the bank's application page in the digital and forum assets | Shows ROI |
| Group add-on (optional) | CNBC Indonesia for a finance-heavy audience | Extra reach to investors and business owners |

**4. KPIs to report:** TV GRP and reach against the target audience, video views and completion rate, article reads, clicks and leads, forum attendance.

**5. Commercial logic:** build the package from the rate card, apply the package discount within your authority, add value through bonus digital or social placements rather than cutting price, agree payment terms, and confirm who signs off (brand and agency).

**6. Close:** *"If we lock this in by [date], we can secure the segment for the launch month. Shall I prepare the final schedule?"*

**Practise the same structure for:** an automotive launch (your Auto Show experience), an FMCG beverage (the biggest spending category), and a regional government or BUMD in Kalimantan that wants to promote investment.

## 13. Glossary: Indonesian media sales terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Rate card** | The official price list (per spot, per package). Real deals are usually discounted from it |
| **Spot** | A single TV commercial airing, usually 15s or 30s |
| **Prime time** | Highest-viewing hours, roughly 18:00–22:00 |
| **TVR** (TV rating) | % of the target audience watching a programme on average |
| **Share** | % of people watching TV at that moment who are watching this channel or programme |
| **GRP** (gross rating points) | Sum of the TVRs of all spots in a campaign: total weight |
| **Reach / frequency** | Reach is the % of the audience who saw the ad at least once; frequency is the average number of times they saw it |
| **CPRP** | Cost per rating point = spot cost ÷ TVR. Agencies use it to compare TV efficiency |
| **CPM** | Cost per 1,000 impressions (digital) |
| **Programme sponsorship / title sponsor** | A brand attached to a programme: "presented by", opening and closing billboards |
| **Built-in (BI) / product placement** | The brand integrated into programme content |
| **Adlib** | A presenter or anchor mentions the brand live |
| **Squeeze frame** | The programme shrinks on screen while an ad fills the frame around it |
| **Running text / super(impose)** | Scrolling text or an on-screen logo during a programme |
| **Virtual ad** | A digitally inserted brand graphic in the studio or on screen |
| **Blocking time** | Buying an entire time slot for a client-produced or sponsored programme |
| **Advertorial / branded content** | Paid content in an editorial style. Must be labelled as sponsored |
| **Display, pre-roll, native** | Digital formats: banners, video ads before content, ads styled like content |
| **Programmatic / PMP** | Automated digital ad buying; a private marketplace is a programmatic deal with selected buyers |
| **Brief** | The client's or agency's request: objective, target, budget, period, KPIs |
| **Media plan** | The agency's channel and schedule plan |
| **PO / IO** | Purchase order or insertion order: the formal booking |
| **Materi iklan / material order** | The ad creative and its scheduling instructions |
| **Traffic** | The team that schedules ads into the broadcast log |
| **Bukti tayang / proof of airing** | Evidence the ad aired as booked (broadcast log or certificate) |
| **Make-good / replacement spot** | A free re-run when an ad didn't air as booked |
| **Agency commission** | The share of the media cost the agency keeps (commonly around 15% in traditional media) |
| **Agency volume bonus / rebate** | An incentive to an agency for total volume across the year |
| **TOP (term of payment)** | Agreed payment period, e.g. 30, 60 or 90 days |
| **Faktur pajak** | Indonesian VAT invoice, needed for the client's tax records |
| **AR / collection** | Accounts receivable: money owed and chasing payment |
| **Annual deal / upfront** | A client or agency commits a yearly budget in exchange for better terms |
| **ATL / BTL** | Above the line (mass media) / below the line (events, activations, direct) |
| **Off-air** | Events and activities outside the broadcast (awards, forums, roadshows) |
