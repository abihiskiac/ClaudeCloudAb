# Style Guide – Weekly Market Overview (Bahasa Indonesia → English)

Use this guide when translating the Weekly Market Overview from Bahasa Indonesia into English. The goal is a faithful translation that keeps the flow of the original, written in the past tense, with numbers in English format.

## Delivery

- Deliver the translation as plain text first. Convert it to a Word document only when asked.

## Structure and flow

- Keep the page markers ("Page 1", "Page 2", …) and the paragraph and sentence order of the original. Do not merge, split or reorder points.
- Each page opens with a two- to three-sentence summary paragraph.
- Each section starts with a short title followed by a full stop, then the body text runs on in the same paragraph.
  - Example: `Energy Tragedy. In the week ending 16 October 2026, Brent rose 4.91%…`
- Section titles are always in English. Render the meaning of the Indonesian title as a short, natural English headline (e.g. "Running Hot", "Haven Bid", "Exit Math", "The Sunshine Behind the Heavy Rain").
- Inline lists use numbered items with a closing bracket, each starting with a capital letter and separated by semicolons:
  - `This was driven by: 1) Attacks on vessels…; 2) The Houthis continued…; 3) Ukraine resumed…`
- Sub-points use `➢`, a label and a colon:
  - `➢ Earnings: Several semiconductor producers reported…`
- Keep chart references in brackets: `(Exhibit 1)`.

## Tense

- Write everything in the past tense, including forward-looking statements:
  - "could become stickier", "were expected to help contain volatility", "were likely to be led by", "was estimated at", "planned to launch", "were scheduled to report", "could prompt".

## Numbers

| Item | Format | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Percentages | Two decimals | 4.91%, 783.00%, 3.70% YoY |
| Prices | Currency code, space, two decimals, unit | USD 109.86/barrel |
| Index levels | Two decimals | DXY 101.84, VIX 19.99 |
| Large amounts | Currency code, two decimals, word for scale | USD 1.50 billion, KRW 107.40 trillion, TWD 763.10 billion |
| Basis points | Whole number + "bps" | down 8 bps, rising 15 bps |
| Multiples | Two decimals + "x" | 2.77x |
| IDR exchange rate | Comma thousands separator, no decimals | 18,012 |
| Ranges | En dash | 10.40–14.91% |
| Prior-period comparison | In brackets after the figure | (Aug-2026: 2.40%), (9-Oct-26: 70.30%) |
| Time spans in prose | Spelled out | five-month high, four days, five years, two levels |
| Counts of items | Numerals | 9 of 18 constituents, the 9 MSCI constituents |

- Use English number formatting: full stop as the decimal separator, comma as the thousands separator (Indonesian `4,91%` → `4.91%`, `18.012` → `18,012`).

## Dates and periods

- Full dates: `16 October 2026`
- Months: `September 2026`
- Quarters: `Q3 2026`
- Inside comparison brackets: `Aug-2026` for months, `9-Oct-26` for specific days
- Common phrasing: "in the week ending 16 October 2026", "the highest since 19 May 2026", "since the start of the year"

## Terminology

- Abbreviations: YoY, YTD, P/E, bps, FOMC, the Fed, DXY, VIX, SOX
- Tenors: UST 2YR, UST 10YR, FR 10YR
- Indonesian terms keep their local acronym. Explain them in English the first time they appear:
  - "Indonesian government bonds (SBN)", "Bank Indonesia (BI)"
- IHSG → JCI
- FR, PBS, IDR and stock tickers (BBCA, BBRI, BMRI, TLKM, ASII) stay as they are.
- Use "respectively" for paired lists ("dropped 13.97%, 20.75%, and 4.72%, respectively").
- Use the serial (Oxford) comma.

## Spelling and typography

- American spelling (e.g. "capitalization").
- Curly apostrophes and quotes (’ “ ”).

## Reference sample

An approved example of the style:

> Oil prices climbed to a five-month high amid escalating geopolitical tensions. Meanwhile, core CPI rose in tandem with Brent, raising the likelihood of another Fed hike in December 2026. However, UST yields fell on attractive yield levels and risk-off sentiment.
>
> Energy Tragedy. In the week ending 16 October 2026, Brent rose 4.91% to USD 109.86/barrel (the highest since 19 May 2026) and WTI rose 10.41% to USD 101.41/barrel (the highest since 17 September 2026). This was driven by: 1) Attacks on vessels in the Strait of Hormuz in October reached their highest number since the Iran war began; 2) The Houthis continued their attacks on Saudi Arabia, including Yanbu, the Kingdom’s alternative export route to Hormuz; 3) Ukraine resumed strikes on Russian refineries, just four days after Trump announced an energy ceasefire.
>
> Running Hot. US CPI rose 3.70% YoY in September 2026 and PPI rose 6.30% YoY, the highest since December 2022. Higher oil prices also fed into the prices of other goods and services, with core CPI rising to 2.60% YoY (Aug-2026: 2.40%) (Exhibit 1). Meanwhile, with Brent already 7.69% above its September 2026 average (USD 102.02/barrel), inflation could become stickier in October 2026. As a result, the probability of a Fed hike to 4.25% in December 2026 rose to 77.30% (9-Oct-26: 70.30%), while the probability for the October 2026 FOMC fell to 15.50%, as the Fed had just raised rates in September 2026.
>
> The Sunshine Behind the Heavy Rain. Although global tech stocks corrected sharply over the past week, several fundamental catalysts were expected to help contain volatility going forward:
>
> ➢ Earnings: Several semiconductor producers reported robust Q3 2026 earnings: 1) Samsung’s operating profit reached KRW 107.40 trillion (+783.00% YoY); 2) TSMC’s net income reached TWD 763.10 billion (+69.00% YoY); 3) ASML’s net income came in at EUR 5.00 billion (+138.00% YoY). Other semiconductor producers, such as NVIDIA and SK Hynix, were also scheduled to report earnings at the end of October 2026.
>
> ➢ Macro drivers: China’s Q3 2026 GDP grew 4.50% YoY, above the 4.40% consensus. Exports were the main contributor, growing 25.20% YoY in August 2026. Within exports, growth was driven by high-tech components, which surged 59.20% YoY.
>
> Exit Math. Trading on the JCI was briefly halted on 16 October 2026, and the index closed 11.57% lower, its steepest daily decline since January 1998 (Exhibit 5). The 9 MSCI constituents fell 10.40–14.91% and accounted for around 29% of the JCI’s market-cap decline. Should the reclassification to standalone market proceed, passive fund outflows were estimated at around USD 1.50 billion, while active fund exposure reached USD 4.37 billion, nearly matching total foreign net selling in Indonesian equities in 2026 of USD 4.89 billion (Exhibit 6).
>
> Bond Buffer. Outflow pressure weakened the IDR by 0.72% to 18,012 in the week ending 16 October 2026, near its record low of 18,178 on 8 June 2026. This could prompt Bank Indonesia (BI) to tighten monetary policy more aggressively. In addition, IDR weakness was a key driver of FR volatility, with the FR 10YR yield rising 15 bps to 7.36%, the highest since July 2026.
